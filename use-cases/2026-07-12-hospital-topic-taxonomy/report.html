What this run establishes

It identifies a coherent vocabulary for hospital experience analysis: the major dimensions people discuss and how fragmented source wording can be consolidated into stable topics.

What still needs another step

To say how common each topic is, compare facilities, or chart topic sentiment over time, each review must be assigned one or more final topics. Discovery-stage topic counts cannot substitute for review-level prevalence.

Overall interpretation

The run produced a concise, operational taxonomy spanning access, staff interactions, administration, facilities, clinical care, coordination, patient rights and specialty care. The taxonomy defines what can be measured in the subsequent review-labelling step; it does not by itself show which topics are most prevalent.

Next analytical step: label reviews with the 29 final topics, then calculate topic prevalence, sentiment or rating by facility, state and year. That is the point at which the taxonomy becomes a measurement system and supports reliable time-series or geographic comparisons.
